I recently watched a short video of a primary school teacher in Igboora, a man in his late fifties. He had just finished a plate of amala, someone was beating a drum, and he was dancing. Fully. Joyfully. Completely at home in the moment.

Part of me smiled. Another part of me felt something heavier. From what I could see, here was a man capable of far more than where life had placed him. Not because teaching is small — teaching is one of the most important jobs there is — but because I sensed a mind that the life around him had never fully stretched.

I don't know his whole story. Maybe that moment is everything he ever wanted. But watching him, a question came to me that had less to do with him and more to do with me: *Am I living at the level I'm capable of, or have I just gotten comfortable where I am?*

That question has followed me since. Everywhere I look I see people living well below what they can do — and the hardest part is that most of them don't realize it. Sometimes, I suspect, that includes me.

## First, a fair objection

Maybe that dance *is* his peak, and who am I to say otherwise? I take that possibility seriously. Joy is not a failure, and peace is not something to be pitied. Plenty of people chase "more" their whole lives and never once dance like that.

So let me be precise about what bothers me. It isn't that people are content. It's that many people are content by default — not because they looked at the options and chose this, but because they never saw that there were options. A choice you didn't know you had is not really a choice.

## Why people settle

### They can't see further than what's around them.

You cannot aim for what you have never seen. If everyone in your world lives a certain way, that way quietly becomes the edge of what's possible. Many people don't lack ambition. They lack exposure.

### Comfort doesn't hurt.

Pain pushes us to move. Comfort doesn't. When the bills are paid and the days are familiar, there is no alarm going off — so nothing changes. Years can pass that way.

### Growth feels like failure at first.

Going further means walking into rooms where you feel unqualified, learning things that make you feel slow, and trying things that might not work in front of people who know you. Staying where you are protects your pride. It just costs you everything you could have become.

### They think they already know enough.

This one is the most dangerous, because it can happen to anyone — including people who have already grown a lot. The moment you believe you've arrived, you stop moving.

## The loop that keeps you growing

I remember the first time I truly realized how much I didn't know. It was humbling, and it was the beginning of everything good that came after. But here is the part I keep reminding myself of:

> Just as you realized how much you didn't know after you came to know — now that you know, still know that you don't know it all.

That is the loop. Learn something. Let it show you how much more there is. Stay humble about it. Learn again. Humility isn't the opposite of ambition — it is what keeps ambition alive. The person who knows they don't know everything will never build a ceiling and call it home.

## Where to start

1. **Widen what you see.** Read, travel if you can, and spend time with people who are a few steps ahead of you. Let them stretch your idea of what's normal.
2. **Ask yourself honestly:** is my comfort a choice I made, or a place I drifted into?
3. **Pick one thing to learn this month** that makes you feel a little uncomfortable — and finish it.
4. **Pass it on.** Somebody around you has never seen what you've seen. Show them. Exposure is a gift you can give.

## A last word

I hope that teacher keeps dancing. Truly. My wish isn't that he had been less happy — it's that every person, wherever they start, gets the chance to see how far they could go, and then decide for themselves.

We all start small. The tragedy is only in mistaking the starting line for the finish. Keep learning, stay humble, and live for more.
